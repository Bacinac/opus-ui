Player is one interface for a television, a computer, a tablet and a phone. The shape of the screen is chosen from what the device can do, its pointer and its width, and not from what it says it is. On a television everything is done with the remote.

## Home and the shelves

**Home** puts together rows from the library at the moment it opens: **Continue watching**, **Continue listening**, **Recently added**, **Most played artists**. Player keeps no catalogue and asks Library for everything, so nothing there can be out of date.

The **Films**, **Series** and **Music** sections each have their pages:

- **All films**, **All series**, **All artists**: the whole shelf, sorted by when it was added, by title or by year.
- **Trending**, **Award winners**, **Genres**, **Actors**, **Studios**: what exists in the world, not only on your shelf. Beside a film you do not have, it says which of the services you subscribe to already has it.
- **Radio**: radio stations, shown as records on a shelf.
- **Search**: a search by exact title or name.

Every shelf says how much is on it and how many hours that takes. Artists are filed by their name, not their article: The Doors stand under D. The shelves are the same for everyone; what is kept per person is only how far they got and what they have watched and listened to.

## What you do not have

A film, a series or an album that is not on the shelf can be asked for with **Get it**, and a series with **Get all** or **Get season**. The request goes into Library's queue and from there takes the same road as everything else. What is asked for is one film, one series or one album, never an artist's whole career. A guest cannot ask for anything.

## Playback

Player remembers how far each profile got and carries on from there. In a series it offers **Skip intro**, and at the end of an episode **Next episode**. The language of sound and subtitles is chosen by your profile's order of languages, and the look of the subtitles is set for each device on its own.

While a film plays, **Ends at** says when it will end, and **Details** shows what is playing right now: the codec and the picture, the frame rate, what is sent and at what rate, the screen and its refresh rate, and how the connection is holding. **Show more** opens the whole story, the cast and the server and device details.

Before a film starts, the device measures how fast the server reaches it. A film that needs more than two thirds of that is re-encoded by the server's graphics card to the screen's size at 70 % of the connection, and a film that stops twice within two minutes waiting for data carries on from there at half the width. A steady picture comes before the highest quality.

With an album playing, **Lyrics** shows the words of the song that is on, and pressing a line takes the song to it.

A television decodes the picture in hardware. When it cannot, playback reports an error rather than quietly loading the processor.

## To the television and to the DAC

From a phone or a computer a film is sent with **Cast to a TV**: it carries on in OPUS on the television from the same moment, and the phone keeps an **On the television** bar as a remote. Music has an **Output** menu: this device, the DAC on the playback server, which is given the original sound, DSD included, or the television for multichannel sound. The house's announcements are spoken over the music on the DAC, and the music then carries on where it stopped.
