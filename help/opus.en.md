OPUS is a household collection of music, films, series, web video and photographs. It is made of three modules. Each is an application of its own, with its own address and its own release, and they share the sign-in, the look and the words. The module switcher in the top bar opens another module in its own window.

## Three modules

- **Library** decides what is worth having, what counts as having it and what a file is called. It follows artists and series, imports and names what arrives, says what is missing and keeps the photo catalogue. It also holds the accounts of everyone in the household.
- **Downloads** acquires. It searches indexers, downloads through Usenet, torrent and Soulseek, fetches web video and hands the finished file over in the landing zone together with its path. That is where its job ends.
- **Player** is what the household uses every day: films, series, music, radio and the family photographs, on a television, a computer or a phone.

## Judgement is kept apart from mechanism

Library decides which release is worth taking, whether what arrived passes the check and what the file will be called. Downloads only carries it out and knows nothing of albums or subtitles. So Library holds no address or key of any download engine, only the address of Downloads.

Player keeps no catalogue. It asks Library about everything that exists at the moment it needs it, so nothing on its screens can be out of date. What belongs to it is only what belongs to the viewer: how far each person got, the profile and the device's settings.

## Who sees which module

Library and Downloads are for maintaining the install, and the administrator sees all of them. A user sees only the photographs in Library, and a guest has nothing there. Player is for everyone.
