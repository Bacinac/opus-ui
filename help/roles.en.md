Everyone in the household has an account in Library. Downloads and Player do not copy that list but ask for it, so a name changed in one place changes everywhere. The administrator keeps the list: in Library, on their account page, under **People**.

## Three standings

- An **admin** maintains the install. They see all three modules: Library's settings and download queue, the Downloads engines and Player's **Server and profiles** page. An install always has at least one admin, and nobody can remove themselves.
- A **user** is a member of the household. In Player they watch and listen to everything, see the family photographs and have a vault of their own, and they can ask for a film, a series or an album that is missing. In Library they see only the photographs.
- A **guest** is somebody the household trusted with the address, not with the family album. They see films, series and music, but not the download queue or anyone's vault, and they cannot ask for anything. They see photographs only once the admin, in Library under **People**, has chosen **Whose photographs they see**: then only those with one of those people in them, and the search knows only their names and places. With nobody chosen they see no photograph at all. The cameras and the quiz stay closed to a guest.

A password has at least eight characters, because the vault's key is derived from it.

## Signing in

The same account holds in all three modules. You sign in with a username and password, or with a **passkey**, which opens OPUS with a fingerprint, a face or the device's PIN. A passkey is added in Library, on the account page.

## A profile: who is watching

Player starts by asking **Who is watching?**. A profile is picked, not proved: a television has only the arrow keys of its remote, and the profile decides only whose half-watched films these are. What somebody may do is decided by the sign-in.

Everyone with an account is offered as a profile, with the face the admin chose for them in Library under **People** as **Face in the photographs**. A profile remembers how far each person got, the order of languages for sound and subtitles, and what the screensaver shows. Profiles come only from Library: Player makes nobody of its own, so somebody without an account is not offered. When only one person is on offer, Player picks them by itself. The admin account is not offered as a profile: it is for maintaining the install, not for watching.

## A television is let in with a code

A television is not a person but a device of the household, and nobody is signed in on it. When OPUS is first opened on a television, a code appears on the screen. The admin, in Library on the account page, types that code under **Devices** into **Code from the screen**, gives the device a name and chooses **Let a box in**. The code renews itself after a few minutes. Access can be taken back in the same place, and each device says which build of the app it runs.

A box can be somebody's. Under **Devices**, in **Whose box**, a box is tied to a person: it then does not ask who is watching but comes in as that person, with their profile and only what that person may see. A box with no owner belongs to the household and offers every profile. When the person leaves the roster, their box's access goes with them, and the box is let in again with a code.
