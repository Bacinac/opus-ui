OPUS holds five kinds: **music**, **films**, **series**, **web video** and **photographs**. The kind strip at the top of a page changes not only what the page shows but its whole vocabulary. An artist has releases and tracks, a film has subtitles, a series has seasons and episodes, and a photograph has a date, a place and the people in it.

## The same verbs, different nouns

Following, acquiring, importing and "what is missing" work the same for every kind, but each speaks its own words and has its own states. An album can be "In library (8/12)", and a film "Waiting for subtitles". The download queue shows every kind in one list, and each row keeps its own kind's states.

## A kind appears on its own

A kind exists when its folder is really there on the server. An install with only music is a music app: there is not a single word about films in it, and nothing had to be switched off. When a films folder is added at install, films appear.

## Where the data comes from

- **Music**: Deezer is the main catalogue, with Spotify and Discogs beside it. Wikidata settles an artist's identity, and MusicBrainz serves only as evidence of an edition.
- **Films and series**: TMDB. Subtitles come from the file itself, from a subtitle file beside it, or from OpenSubtitles.
- **Web video**: a link to a video, a channel or a playlist, fetched by yt-dlp.
- **Photographs**: read where they are, in the household's folder.
